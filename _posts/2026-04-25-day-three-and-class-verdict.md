Day Three of the 2026 NFL Draft is where front offices either quietly compound their advantages or surrender them. The Baltimore Ravens, on the evidence of seven selections across rounds four through seven, did the former.

The headline Day Three move was the trade up to take SMU tight end Matt Hibner at pick one hundred and thirty-three. Hibner gives the offence a second tight-end body that can both block in-line and threaten the seam, freeing Mark Andrews from being the only player on the field who triggers a defensive adjustment. In the fifth round, the Ravens took Duke cornerback Chandler Rivers and Alabama tight end Josh Cuevas, before owner Steve Bisciotti reportedly used his customary one-pick personal veto to take Clemson running back Adam Randall at pick one hundred and seventy-four (Pressbox, 2026). Randall's tape suggests a contact-balance back in the mould of the team's preferred profile.

The remaining picks — Michigan State punter Ryan Eckley, Michigan defensive tackle Rayshaun Benny, and Northwestern guard Evan Beerntsen — are unlikely to produce starters in 2026. They are roster-construction picks: special-teams contributors, depth pieces, camp competition. That is precisely what a Day Three should look like. Reaching for projected starters this late tends to produce neither.

The aggregate verdict, then. Pro Football Focus graded the class a B-plus and ranked the Ravens sixth overall in wins-above-average added through the draft (Pro Football Focus, 2026). Baltimore Beatdown's grade-by-grade rundown was similarly positive on the line investment but warned that the class lacks an obvious immediate impact at safety, where a need still lingers (Baltimore Beatdown, 2026). College Football News flagged the Hibner trade as the boldest mid-round move of any AFC North team (College Football News, 2026).

For a roster transitioning to a new head coach, this is a sensible class. It addresses the offensive line, the pass rush, and the perimeter at the receiver position. It defers the safety question to free agency or 2027. And it gives Jesse Minter eleven new players to fit into a culture he is still in the process of defining.

The 2026 season will tell us how much of this analysis survives contact with reality. The draft, on its own merits, was a quietly strong one.

*Sources: Baltimore Ravens (2026c, 2026e); ESPN (2026b); NFL.com (2026).*
