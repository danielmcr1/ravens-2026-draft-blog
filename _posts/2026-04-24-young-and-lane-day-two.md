Day Two of the 2026 NFL Draft saw the Baltimore Ravens add two players who address the weakest two units on last year's roster. With the forty-fifth overall pick, the franchise selected Missouri edge rusher Zion Young. With the eightieth pick, they took USC wide receiver Ja'Kobi Lane.

Young's selection answers a question the Ravens defence has been quietly asking for two seasons: where does the next consistent pass-rush presence come from? The unit ranked in the middle of the league in pressure rate in 2025, and Young's profile — long arms, strong first step, comfortable setting an edge against the run — fits the hybrid front that defensive coordinator Zach Orr is expected to continue running. He is not a finished product. He will need a season behind the established starters to refine his hand usage. But the ceiling justifies the second-round investment.

Lane is the more interesting selection from a strategic standpoint. The Ravens have struggled for years to find a receiver who can win consistently outside the numbers without help from scheme. Lane, at six-foot-three with documented forty-yard speed in the four-four range, gives Lamar Jackson a true vertical threat that the offence has lacked since the trade of Marquise Brown. CBS Baltimore's draft coverage flagged the pick as one of the higher-upside selections of the second day across the league (CBS Baltimore, 2026).

Whether Lane plays a hundred snaps in his rookie season or six hundred will depend on how quickly he picks up the route tree. But the talent is real, and the need was acute. On both counts, Day Two graded out well.

*Sources: Baltimore Ravens (2026); CBS Baltimore (2026); NFL.com (2026).*
